Many sales slow down or fall through not because of the price, but because of missing or irregular documents discovered too late, when the buyer has already applied for a mortgage. Preparing everything before publishing the listing saves weeks. Here is the basic checklist; your specific case should always be confirmed with the notary.

## Property documents

- **Title deed**: the deed through which you became the owner (purchase, gift, inheritance).
- **Land registry search and floor plan**: they must match the real state of the home. A moved room or a closed-off storage space without updating the land registry are discrepancies that almost always emerge at the deed stage.
- **Energy performance certificate (APE)**: required to sell, and the class must appear in the listing. It is prepared by a qualified technician.
- **Building permits and planning compliance**: licences, concessions, permits and amnesties relating to the construction and to works carried out over the years.
- **Certificates of the systems**, if available, and habitability.

## Condominium documents

- **Condominium regulations** and ownership shares.
- **Ordinary expenses** and the status of payments, to show that you are up to date.
- **Extraordinary works** that have been voted on or already approved, to be explained to the buyer and not hidden.

## Personal documents

- Identity document and tax code of all the owners.
- If there is still an active mortgage, the repayment schedule and the outstanding debt: the lien has to be handled and cancelled at the deed.

## Why do it first

A buyer who finds everything in order decides quickly and negotiates less on the price. If a discrepancy emerges, correcting it right away costs less than doing it with a preliminary contract already signed. And if you do not know where to start, a preliminary check of the documents tells you what is missing before you go on the market.
