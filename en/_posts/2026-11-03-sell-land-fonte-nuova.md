> **In short:** To sell land in Fonte Nuova you first need to work out whether it is agricultural or buildable, because price, taxes and buyers all depend on it. Then you need the urban planning certificate (CDU), requested from the municipality, and your land registry records in order. If the land is agricultural, agricultural pre-emption rights may also apply.

## The land market in Fonte Nuova

Fonte Nuova includes areas such as Tor Lupara and Santa Lucia, close to the Via Nomentana and the edge of Rome. In an area closely tied to commuting, open plots appeal mostly to people looking for land close to Rome or a piece of ground to keep.

## Agricultural or buildable: the first question

Land is not buildable because the listing says so: the municipality's town plan decides. Agricultural land appeals mainly to people who farm or want a plot to keep; a building plot appeals to those who want to build and is usually worth more, but it follows different tax rules. The CDU puts the designation in writing and spares you arguments with the buyer.

## Documents to prepare before selling

- Title deed (purchase, inheritance or gift)
- Up-to-date land registry search and map extract
- Urban planning certificate (CDU), requested from the municipality where the land is
- Land registry subdivision, if you sell only part of it
- Clear boundaries and access (public road or right of way)
- Any farm leases, restrictions, easements or mortgages

## The urban planning certificate (CDU)

The CDU states what can be done on the land under the town planning instruments. For deeds selling land it must be attached to the deed (art. 30 of Presidential Decree 380/2001), with some exceptions, for example certain plots that are appurtenances of buildings and fall below a given area: your notary will check. The municipality issues it within 30 days of the request and it is valid for one year from issue, as long as the planning instruments do not change in the meantime. It is best to request it before listing the land, so that buyer and notary start from a certain fact.

## Agricultural pre-emption: when it applies

If the land is agricultural, the law gives a right of pre-emption: first to whoever farms it under a lease as a direct farmer (normally for at least two years) and, if there is no tenant, to the direct farmer who owns neighbouring land. If the sale is not notified as the law requires, the person with the right can buy the land back even after the deed is signed. Before signing a preliminary contract, ask your notary whether anyone has such a right in your case.

## Taxes: buildable or agricultural

For a private seller, the capital gain on buildable land is always taxable, even if you have owned it for more than five years or inherited or received it as a gift (art. 67 of the TUIR). On agricultural land the gain is generally taxable only if you sell within five years of buying. The taxes on the deed depend on the type of land and on who is buying (for example a direct farmer or a professional agricultural entrepreneur). For your specific case, ask a notary or an accountant.

## How the price of land is set

The price depends on the planning designation and, for a building plot, on how much the plan allows to be built. Then come area and shape, access, distance from roads and services, utilities, slope and exposure and any restrictions. For agricultural land the crop (olive grove, arable, vegetable plot) and water availability also count. Two neighbouring plots can be worth very different amounts: that is why a site visit is needed.

## What buyers look at in Fonte Nuova

Buyers here look at the distance from Rome and the Nomentana, access and what the town plan allows. Clear information about the land's designation avoids long negotiations.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I sell land without a CDU?**
Generally not: for land the CDU must be attached to the deed of sale, with exceptions, and without it the deed may be void. Request it from the municipality before listing.

**Can I sell only part of the land?**
In principle yes, but a land registry subdivision is needed. If the subdivision is meant to create building plots, be careful: the law prohibits unauthorised land development (art. 30 of Presidential Decree 380/2001). Ask the municipality and a surveyor before subdividing.

**Is inherited land taxed when I sell it?**
If it is buildable, the capital gain is taxable even if you inherited it; if it is agricultural, generally only if you sell within five years of acquiring it. For your case, ask a notary or an accountant.
